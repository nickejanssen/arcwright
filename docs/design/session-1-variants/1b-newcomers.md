# Version 1b: Newcomers

Part of [the version overview](README.md). The founder approved the list of versions and the skill names on 20 November 2026 ("Approve versions and names", issue #429). The details below are still open to his edits. The founder chose this as the fourth version ("Newcomers instead", answer 1) over a bluffing version, when asked which kind of player the fourth should be built for.

## What it is

The same case with the fewest things to learn. Fewer mini-games, no racing, no final race, and a host that says in plain words what to do at each step. The case, the investigating and Last Call are unchanged.

## Who it targets

Friends who rarely play games and may not know what a "round" or a "mini-game" is. Group 2 in the run order plays it first, and group 1 plays it second.

## How it is composed

| Part | Setup (a starting point, not a rule; D-145) |
| --- | --- |
| Round 1 | Scene, investigate, then Search and Find (control), explained step by step |
| Round 2 | Scene and investigate only; no mini-game (zero slots is allowed, D-147) |
| Round 3 | Scene, investigate, then Battle of Wits with easy questions, one round |
| Final race | None |
| Last Call and ending | Unchanged, with a plain-words explanation line before each step |
| Pacing | Longer phase clocks where the arc's pacing setting allows |

Two mini-games in three rounds, `mini_game_pick: fitting`, so no mechanic repeats (D-136, D-147).

## The goal

Prove that a group who does not know the game can play it start to finish with nobody coaching them. That is the thing session 3 (friends without the founder) depends on.

## How it is different from the others

| Compared with | Difference |
| --- | --- |
| 1a Agency race | Half the games, no race, no repeat, much more explanation |
| 1c Story and intrigue | No solo find and no story-first pacing; the lightness is in the rules, not the tone |
| 1d Deduction and puzzles | No hard questions or puzzles; easy ones only |

It is a difficulty and onboarding setting more than a different kind of fun. It earns a place as its own version because a different kind of player (non-gamers) is the target, and the founder asked for it.

## What is being tested

- Can a first-time player follow the game without asking the host? Every survey counts host questions, so this version has a baseline from the others.
- Does a lighter setup still feel like being a detective?
- Is it fun for people who do not like racing?
- Does the host's wording, not the rules, carry the teaching?

## Why this variation

The pass bar (fun, felt like a detective, play again) is meaningless if players are confused. Session 3 has nobody to help. The founder picked it for that reason, over a bluffing version, which was the most different from the others but costs the hardest new mechanic. It is also the cheapest of the four: it reuses 1a's mini-games and mostly removes things.

## Mini-games and side games

| Role | Game | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Control | Search and Find, round 1 | New; shared with every version |
| Cohort | Battle of Wits, easy questions, round 3 | New; shared with 1a and (logic only) 1d |
| Side games | None | |

Alternates (see [the candidate list](minigame-slotting.md)): The Witness Protection Sketch Artist, a funny no-wrong-answer game, and The Emergency Seating Committee on paper.

## What the survey adds

The shared questions, plus "Could you follow what to do without help? (yes, mostly, no)". Host questions are counted in every version.

## What it costs

| Part | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| Arc setup | Arc file only (verified: slots, `fitting` and zero slots exist after AW-344) |
| Mini-games | Search and Find and Battle of Wits, both shared with 1a |
| Plain-words host lines | Writing: one line per phase kind. Host lines are written by a person (spec 0094) |
| Easy question bank | Writing, a small set |

## Ready if (5 December)

1a is built (1b adds nothing new to build), the easy questions and the extra host lines are written, and the version name is saved in the play record.

## What it looks like (invented example)

The host says at the start of each phase what to do: "Tap one place to look into. You have six looks in the whole game." In round 1 everyone gets the same picture of a hotel lobby: "Find the green umbrella. Tap it when you see it." No clock bar shows until the second time a phase appears. Round 2 is a scene and investigating only. In round 3, "Which colour do you get by mixing blue and yellow?" At Last Call the host says: "Pick who, how and why from what you found. Nobody sees it until the end."

## Prior art

Jackbox: phones as the only controller and a host who explains each round is why non-gamers join (recalled, not checked). *Exit: The Game* and *Unlock!* print a difficulty rating on each scenario so a group can pick an easier one (recalled). Inference: a lower-difficulty version helps only if the host's wording carries the teaching; this version tests that.
