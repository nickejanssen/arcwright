# Nightcap — Minigames & Competitive Pulse

**Status:** Foundational direction DECIDED; implementation details TESTING  
**Type:** GDD system one-pager

## Purpose

Minigames are Nightcap's **competitive pulse**, not its strategic center.

They create skill expression, spectacle, rivalry, and volatile tactical swings while remaining subordinate to the murder investigation.

## Core Laws

- Story-wrapped without sacrificing mechanical fun.
- Broad eccentric-investigator / spy skill portfolio.
- Same competitive challenge for players in V1; no hidden outcome manipulation.
- Graduated ranking.
- **Big swings, short half-life.**
- Speed is not a standard endgame tie-breaker.

## Diegetic Action Rule

A major Nightcap minigame should normally feel like something the detective is **actually doing in the fiction**, for example searching a scene, reconstructing a damaged record, comparing samples, restoring a recording, tracing a timeline, or handling another case-specific investigative problem.

Do not present a generic abstract mode, tray, meter, or challenge with unexplained system vocabulary when the player lacks a fictional and mechanical reason for why it exists.

Before first use, the game should make clear in plain contextual language:

- what the player is doing;
- what success/failure can change;
- what is being contested, if anything; and
- what the result changes when the player returns to the investigation.

This teaches the mechanic without explaining which deduction the player should make afterward.

## Murder Relevance

Major competitive beats must reconnect to the murder-facing game.

Their outcomes should materially affect something such as:

- investigative opportunity;
- information position;
- access or priority;
- social position;
- Leverage or another finite competitive resource.

Small lightweight competitive moments may exist primarily for energy, humor, or personality when they do not meaningfully interrupt the investigation.

## Reward Boundary

Minigames are **opportunity-first**.

Preferred rewards include:

- priority;
- access;
- temporary advantage;
- resources;
- information;
- control over how an opportunity is used.

A minigame may occasionally expose authored knowledge when fictionally appropriate.

Essential murder proof must still be earned through legitimate investigation. Winning a skill challenge does not substitute for being a good detective.

## Trigger Model

Nightcap uses a **story-triggered hybrid**.

Authored circumstances create natural competitive windows. Player decisions may influence what becomes contested, whether they participate, what opportunity they pursue, or how an earned advantage is used.

Minigames should feel like something happened in the murder story that became competitive, not like a scheduled mode transition.

## Handoff

Preferred loop:

**STORY / INVESTIGATION → COMPETITIVE WINDOW → MINIGAME → FINITE CONSEQUENCE → REACT → MURDER-FACING DECISION**

The return path matters as much as the minigame itself. The player should understand what changed and resume investigation from the new state without the system interpreting the evidence for them.

## Accessibility

Minigames should be accessibility-compatible by construction where practical. Active settings filter the room's eligible minigame pool rather than secretly giving selected players easier competitive outcomes.

## Validation Note

Paper Test #2 v2.2 did **not** establish that minigames are a failed Nightcap pillar. The stronger signal was that the fixture's minigame/resource presentation lacked enough context and narrative integration. Treat the problem as integration and mental-model failure unless later representative evidence indicts the mechanic itself.

## TESTING

- exact minigame frequency;
- reward calibration;
- room-compatible pool breadth;
- player-count configuration;
- story wrapping and transition quality;
- whether major minigames reliably return players to the murder;
- how much rule explanation is needed before a first-use competitive window feels immediately legible.

## Design Checks

> **If the score screen vanished, what changed in the murder game because this competition happened?**

If the answer is nothing, a major minigame is probably disconnected from Nightcap.

> **Could a first-time player explain what they just competed over and what changed afterward without using internal system jargon?**
