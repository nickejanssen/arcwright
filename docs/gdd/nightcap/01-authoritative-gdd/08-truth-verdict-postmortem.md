# Nightcap — The Truth, The Verdict & The Postmortem

**Status:** Foundational direction DECIDED; presentation details TESTING  
**Type:** GDD system one-pager

## Endgame Jobs

Nightcap's closing beats have distinct purposes.

### THE TRUTH — Narrative catharsis

Resolve the canonical murder and let the mystery snap into focus.

### THE VERDICT — Competitive confirmation

Confirm who solved the murder and, only when necessary, surface the small decisive difference that determined the competitive result.

### THE POSTMORTEM — Social catharsis

Reflect how everyone actually behaved as investigators and turn the session into something players want to talk about afterward.

After all theories lock, Nightcap first reveals **only each player's accused culprit**. This is the table's commitment/reaction beat, not a case presentation.

The closing sequence remains:

**THE TRUTH → THE VERDICT → THE POSTMORTEM**

Do not collapse them into one generic results screen.

## The Truth

Nightcap uses an **evidence-led cinematic reconstruction**.

After all final theories are irrevocably locked, the game may finally explain the murder clearly.

The reveal should reconstruct the canonical events, explain critical deception/red herrings, reconnect important moments to discoverable evidence, and resolve misleading information fairly.

The Truth explains **what actually happened**. It should not become a lecture about the reasoning process players should have used.

A cinematic detective-style gathering, proclamation, or confrontation may later augment this beat if testing shows it improves payoff. Any such performance expresses already-resolved state; it does not determine whether a player's theory was correct.

## The Verdict

The result should normally be **obvious from the solve condition**, not defended through a subjective scoring explanation.

- One player solves the murder → that player wins.
- Multiple players solve the murder → they are tied on the murder; use the established competitive tie-break direction.
- Nobody solves the murder → no player is crowned the murder winner; the murderer/case beats the table.

The Verdict should therefore be concise.

When explanation is useful, surface only the decisive factual difference: for example, one player reconstructed an essential event correctly and another did not. Do not walk through every submitted fact, reveal an opaque internal score, or invite a debate over whose argument was more persuasive.

The intended feeling is:

> **I can see what happened and why that result follows.**

not:

> **The game has issued a ruling on whose essay it preferred.**

Detailed audit information may be available after the main payoff if useful for testing, accessibility, or curious players, but it should not dominate the room experience.

## The Postmortem

> **Nightcap doesn't hand out participation awards. It remembers what you did and develops an opinion about you.**

Postmortem characterization uses **truthful exaggeration**.

Actual, observable player behavior determines the underlying characterization. Nightcap may then heighten that truth for comedy without inventing a contradictory personality merely because another joke is funnier. It may not present an unexpressed internal theory, motive, or thought as something the player actually believed. If Nightcap could not observe it, Nightcap does not get to claim it happened.

Relevant behavioral material may include:

- deduction accuracy,
- evidence found or ignored,
- strange obsessions,
- abandoned theories the player actually externalized,
- espionage,
- lies / bluffs,
- alliances / betrayals,
- sharing,
- minigame strengths,
- observation,
- risk,
- confidence,
- speed / decisiveness as behavioral texture (not a standard endgame tie-breaker),
- theory changes the player actually externalized,
- spectacular errors.

## Voice

Original Nightcap comedy: stylish crime confidence, sincere observation punctured by absurdity, dysfunctional affection, painfully specific callbacks, sharp playful insults, and occasional earned profanity.

Do not imitate any living creator's exact prose or signature voice.

## Boundaries

- The competitive result is deterministic and legible.
- Recognition is not alternate victory.
- A session may end with **no player solving the murder**.
- Not every player needs equal postmortem titles, but public story attention during play is deliberately managed elsewhere so players remain visible participants in the room experience.
- Humor targets in-game behavior, not sensitive/personal identity.
- Prefer actual callbacks over canned achievements.
- Endgame presentation must feel like payoff, not essay-writing or a lecture.
