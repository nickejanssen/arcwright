# Nightcap — Player Count & Social Architecture

**Status:** DECIDED + TESTING + OPEN  
**Type:** GDD system one-pager

## Non-negotiable

> **Two-player support is a first-class V1 requirement.**

Do not reframe it as post-V1 or a separate Duel mode.

## Reference Configuration

Four players may remain the current reference configuration for balancing and simulation because it is the best-understood configuration so far. This is not an upper- or lower-bound decision.

## Scaling Law

> **Scale opportunities, not merely runtime, and never support more players by stripping away each player's detective fantasy.**

## Same Fantasy, Different Social Geometry

Nightcap does not attempt to make two people behave like four people.

At two players, the same core fantasy becomes more intimate, directly adversarial, and psychologically focused.

At higher counts, the same systems may create more attribution uncertainty, temporary alliances, information diffusion, coalition risk, and varied social theater.

These are different expressions of the same game rather than separate modes.

## Same Truth, Adapted Opportunities

Player-count scaling preserves:

- canonical culprit,
- canonical murder truth,
- authored core scene / story,
- intellectual Case Challenge,
- proof requirements,
- victory standard.

Player count may selectively adapt:

- information distribution,
- evidence-route density,
- opportunity availability,
- contested windows,
- investigation budget,
- simultaneous interactions,
- Leverage opportunities,
- minigame configuration,
- optional story/social beats.

## Core-Loop Parity

At two players, every major Nightcap pillar remains meaningfully present:

**WATCH → HUNT → THINK → PLAY → REACT → LAST CALL → CASE FILE → THE TRUTH → THE VERDICT → THE POSTMORTEM**

Individual expressions may adapt; major pillars may not become vestigial merely because only two people are playing.

## Two-Player Espionage

Do not manufacture artificial uncertainty when only one opponent exists.

Some actions will naturally be attributable. The uncertainty shifts toward what was learned, copied, protected, left private, or intended.

## Spotlight / Public Attention

**DECIDED:** Nightcap deliberately manages major public story attention across the table, especially as player count rises.

Spotlight means **visible room-level involvement**, not help solving the murder.

Arcwright may distribute public reactions, cinematic beats, social callbacks, or optional visible moments so players receive meaningful opportunities to matter in the shared experience. This must not:

- alter canonical truth,
- change the intellectual Case Challenge,
- hand a player better evidence because they are behind,
- manufacture equal competitive outcomes,
- protect someone from losing,
- force identical airtime regardless of what players do.

The goal is that larger groups still feel like an ensemble mystery rather than a few loud players starring while others disappear from the shared story. Exact Spotlight budgets and distribution rules remain **TESTING**.

## Reference Risks by Count

### 2 players

Strengths: intimate rivalry, low collective coverage, strong individual agency.  
Risks: obvious actor attribution, less coalition chaos, repeated head-to-head minigames, fewer social events.

### 3 players

Strong social uncertainty; major 2-vs-1 risk.

### 4 players

Current best-understood reference: independent investigation, legible behavior, espionage ambiguity, alliances, minigames, and deliberately managed public Spotlight.

### 5–6 players

Potentially viable with explicit scaling; risks include investigation throughput, Spotlight congestion, evidence vacuuming, information overload, and cinematic interruption.

### 7–8 players

Not approved or rejected. Serious risks remain around content burden, collective investigation, individual agency, overlap, and information/social overload.
