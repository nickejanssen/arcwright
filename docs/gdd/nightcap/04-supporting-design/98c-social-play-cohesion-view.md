# Nightcap — Social Play Cohesion View

**Status:** SUPPORTING DESIGN-CONTROL VIEW — not game canon by itself
**Checkpoint:** 05 / Session B adversarial pass

## Purpose

Put Nightcap's player-to-player behavior in one place so social quality, balance, and player-count risks can be audited without turning "social play" into another independent rules subsystem.

Canonical rules remain owned by their authoritative pages. If this view conflicts with one of those pages, the authoritative page and `00-decision-ledger.md` win.

## Social Fantasy

Players are competing detectives, not a cooperative investigation team with a scoring screen added at the end.

The social layer should create:

- rivalry,
- bluffing and selective disclosure,
- interpretation of other investigators' behavior,
- temporary social alignment without formal teams,
- information asymmetry and information warfare,
- opportunities to exploit what another player reveals or overlooks,
- table reactions and memorable interpersonal moments,
- meaningful differences between intimate two-player rivalry and larger-group uncertainty.

It should **not** require constant negotiation, alliance administration, resource accounting, or table policing merely to function.

## Current Cross-System Laws

- Sharing is player-controlled.
- Social bluffing is allowed.
- Alliances are **social only**; Nightcap does not currently define formal teams/contracts.
- Evidence interaction is primarily observe/copy rather than destroy.
- Information warfare may create advantages without converting every advantage into a spendable point.
- Repeated minigame wins must not become the primary gateway to controlling espionage.
- Essential truth cannot be made unfairly unsolvable through PvP manipulation.
- Nightcap does not automatically protect a player because multiple opponents target them. Concentrated competitive pressure is allowed. Intervention is justified only if a repeatable mechanic suppresses the target's ability to continue participating in the investigation; that is treated as a mechanic/playability failure, not as a reason for pity protection or hidden catch-up.
- At two players, do not fake actor anonymity; preserve uncertainty around what the opponent learned, protected, or intends.
- The game may surface social/competitive state but must not tell players what another player's behavior *means* for the murder.

## Balance Risks to Audit Together

### Too little social consequence

Nightcap can degrade into several people independently solving the same single-player mystery.

### Too much formal social machinery

Nightcap can degrade into tracking alliances, currencies, cooldowns, targeting rules, and exceptions instead of investigating a murder.

### Winner snowball

A player who gets ahead at one skill cannot be allowed to repeatedly turn that success into dominant information access across every other system.

### Concentrated targeting

Three or more players can coordinate socially even without formal alliance rules, and Nightcap should not referee that strategy merely because it is lopsided. The failure condition is narrower: a repeatable mechanic must not be chainable in a way that effectively removes someone's ability to keep investigating. Losing advantage is allowed; losing the ability to play is not.

### Accidental cooperation

If all useful information becomes public too easily, competition collapses into collective case solving.

### Social opacity

If private events and espionage are too invisible, players may not perceive rivalry or understand why the room changed. Naturalistic presentation still needs legible cause/effect.

### Table dominance

A loud/confident player must not be able to perform the reasoning for everyone simply through unrestricted shared interfaces or required consensus.

## Use in Future Audits

When changing any social-facing mechanic, check this view first, then edit the actual authoritative owner. Do not add a rule here only because it makes the table look complete.
