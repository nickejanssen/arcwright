# Nightcap v3.1 Creative Review Samples

**Status:** Sample direction and proposed opening framing approved by founder, 2026-10-01. Final rewritten-case review remains pending.  
**Version:** 1.0 | **Date:** 2026-10-01  
**Purpose:** settle the voice and delivery direction for improvements 19-24 before rewriting the fixture.

These are inexpensive text samples, not implemented scenes or approved Nightcap canon. The source is the non-canon _A Knock at Midnight_ fixture. They preserve the murder solution and chronology. See the [implementation contract](../../../specs/0091-nightcap-v3.1-improvements.md).

## Direction and review instructions

**Recommended: restrained period wit with credible pressure.** Keep memorable concrete details and occasional humor, but allow fear, evasion, and silence to sound different. This directly addresses the shared quippy cadence while preserving the fixture's character.

An alternative is a more theatrical, comic ensemble voice. It may support party energy, but risks repeating the same clever register across suspects. The recommendation is the restrained direction; these samples demonstrate it.

Read for voice, emotional credibility, and whether you want to pursue the next question. Mark lines that sound artificial, too explanatory, or emotionally false. Approval establishes a direction for the full draft; it does not certify that external players find it engaging.

These passages use an unnamed written narrator. They do not add generated Vesper lines or bypass the [retained Host authoring contract](../../../design/the-host.md). Production Vesper refrains, performance, and delivery are outside this fixture pass.

## 1. Opening sample

**New framing requiring approval:** the player and Nora accepted Gideon's invitation to witness his promised exposure of Quill. The original source does not establish that invitation. It adds a reason to attend and a bounded competitive relationship, not a new clue or motive.

> Gideon March invited you to the Larkspur to watch him expose a fraud. He invited Nora Vance too. Two detectives, one demonstration. He seemed to enjoy the prospect of an audience that might argue back.
>
> At 11:47, Quill lifts the brass trumpet. Gideon's voice comes through it: "Mrs. Quill, do carry on. I can hear every word."
>
> Quill flinches. Clara is seated with the guests. Rusk leaves the room.
>
> At midnight, Rusk knocks on the writing-room door. No answer. When it opens, Gideon is dead beside his desk.
>
> Nora looks from the body to the corridor. Then she looks at you.
>
> You can begin with the room, Gideon's papers, the séance apparatus, or the people who heard him speak.

**Checks before full use:** align every public opening observation with the existing source; neither the invitation nor Nora's gesture may establish new decisive proof. Put action-budget teaching beside the first choices, outside this story paragraph. Avoid a long rules preamble.

## 2. Pressured interview sample

**Context:** an earned Beatrice challenge. The following first line is appropriate only when the player owns Clara's timeline claim (`e-clara-lie`). Other legitimate triggers need their own known-information prompt, not this line copied indiscriminately.

> "Clara says she never returned to the writing-room corridor after twenty past eleven."
>
> Beatrice looks toward the door.
>
> "I said I saw nothing useful. I was at his door. I had already threatened him; I had no wish to explain why I was there."
>
> She rests her glass on the table.
>
> "I heard Gideon say, 'You sold my work to a parlour trick.' Clara answered. Then I heard a heavy impact. I did not see the blow. I saw Clara leave the corridor. Later she was seated with us when his voice came through."

**What this tests:** she admits the compromising behavior before giving the observation; hearing and sight are distinguished; the player still owns the conclusion. It preserves the existing disclosure rather than splitting it into unnecessary clicks. The alternate Rusk route remains required.

**Authoring check:** do not turn the narrator's staging gestures into notebook evidence. Check that timing/alibi details match the public scene before adding them to spoken testimony.

## 3. Reveal sample

**SPOILERS: read after first play if you want to preserve the solution.** This is a prose excerpt, not the full final reveal or verdict implementation.

> At 11:30, Gideon confronted Clara about the confidential research reaching Quill. Three minutes later she killed him with the brass bookend.
>
> She put his recorded cylinder into Quill's concealed apparatus and was back among the guests by 11:40.
>
> At 11:47, Quill played her usual cue. She did not know the cylinder had been changed. Clara sat with the others while Gideon's voice supplied her alibi.
>
> Rusk found cylinder 43 afterward and hid it to protect the hotel and Quill's fraud. He did not know whose murder he was covering.
>
> Gideon was already dead when you heard him.

The complete reveal must also resolve the innocent suspects' lies and show the player's actual decisive verdict reason. Keep this sequence concise and evidence-led. Do not add production cinematics or a Postmortem system to deliver it.

## Four voices to preserve across the rewrite

| Suspect  | Speech and behavior                                                  | Existing stakes                                             | Avoid                                                  |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Clara    | Precise times, clipped corrections, controlled admissions            | Exposure for leaking Gideon's research                      | Making composure an automatic guilt signal             |
| Quill    | Public theatrical authority that becomes plain speech under pressure | Exposure of fraudulent séances                              | Every response sounding like a comic performance       |
| Rusk     | Practical contingencies and damage control                           | Hotel business tied to Quill's performances                 | Giving him knowledge of the killer he does not possess |
| Beatrice | Blunt admissions with hard limits around what she witnessed          | Threats, letters, and publication of her husband's finances | Converting everything she says into unquestioned fact  |

Sharper employment, debt, family, or social consequences are new authored facts and need explicit consideration. The voice pass cannot invent them silently.
