# Nightcap — Paper Test #2 v2.1 Baseline Revision

**Status:** TEST PROTOCOL REVISION / supporting evidence, not canon  
**Purpose:** Correct v2 Run 01 failure modes before Memory Support comparison.

## Why revise

V2 improved substantially over v1 but failed the Time-to-Fun / detective-feel gate. The player reported only “a little” investigative feeling and only “maybe” wanting another action. Over-signposted formatting and insufficient exploration were the clearest problems.

## Changes from v2

1. **No significance formatting**
   - Bold only names, action labels, and structural headings.
   - Do not bold clue-bearing phrases inside evidence text.
   - Remove “What you know” summaries.

2. **More play before interruption**
   - Increase baseline investigation budget from 3 to 5 actions.
   - Do not force commitment before the player has had opportunity to inspect, trace, and interview.

3. **Interviewing is first-class immediately**
   - Scene, movement, and people are equally visible investigation families from the first decision.
   - Suspects are not presented with motive summaries that pre-rank their relevance.

4. **More diegetic outcomes**
   - Present discoveries as observed objects, spaces, records, or character statements.
   - Avoid interface-authored interpretation of why the discovery matters.

5. **Preserve the game-first rule**
   - No research questions during play.
   - No required written theory.
   - Final commitment remains lightweight.

## Experience target

The player should naturally reach at least one of these reactions before the commitment:

- “Wait, that doesn’t fit.”
- “I want to ask them about this.”
- “I need to check where that leads.”
- “I don’t trust that statement.”

The interface should provide the material; the player supplies the significance.

## Gate

Proceed to Memory Support comparison only when exploratory baseline runs show:

- at least **Mostly** investigative feeling or stronger,
- clear desire to take another investigative action,
- at least one naturally generated theory/question/contradiction impulse,
- no strong report that interface formatting told the player what mattered.
