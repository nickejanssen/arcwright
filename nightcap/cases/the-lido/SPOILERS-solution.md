## 1. The case in brief

- Place: Tilverton Lido, the open air baths of Tilverton, an invented boot-and-shoe town in the English Midlands. Opened 1935 by the old Tilverton Urban District Council. Since the 1974 reorganisation it belongs to Harding Vale District Council, seated nine miles off in Great Harding, which leases it for a peppercorn to the volunteer Tilverton Lido Association.
- Night: Saturday 21 August 1976. The Association's closing-night dance, held on the floor of the let-out pool. Hosepipe ban in force. No moon to speak of.
- Victim: Leonard "Len" Purkiss, 61, solid fuel merchant, chairman of the Association since 1957.
- Culprit: **Dennis Wragg**, 44, the Association's treasurer and tonight's master of ceremonies.

## 2. The trick in one sentence

Nobody brought water to the dead man: the killer quietly let nine feet of it out from under a man who dived off the top board every closing night and never looked down.

The weapon is the absence of water, in the summer the whole country ran out of it.

Plan as Dennis meant it: body found Sunday morning, bone dry in a bone dry pit, a signed District chit saying the tank was empty at six, and two hundred people who heard him say so. "The silly old fool did his dive into an empty pool." He did not allow for Bernard coming with a torch eight minutes later.

Why Dennis: when Len signs the surrender on Monday the books go to audit, and the turnstile has clocked 61,412 admissions in the record summer while the banked takings fit about 44,000. Roughly £2,600 is gone. If Len dies before signing, Miss Gaunt becomes acting chairman, she will never sign, and no auditor comes. The death also looks like hers to want.

## 4. The false story the room believes

"The pool was let out this afternoon. A District chit says the tank was empty at six, the superintendent signed it, and the treasurer told the whole dance. So Len, three gins down, either walked off the edge in the dark or, the old hands mutter, did his daft annual dive and forgot. Which leaves the water on him, and nobody can account for that." Rudge says someone has broken the ban with a hose. Bernard says nothing useful, because he thinks his old valve failed and he killed his friend.

The game's own voice never says the pit was empty before 22:17. It says the pool "was let out at noon" and that the shallow end was dry by teatime, both true. "Dry as a bone" and "empty at six" are said by characters and by a document.

## 7. Suspects

**Dennis Wragg, 44. Treasurer, MC. Chief clerk in the counting house at Corbould's boot factory. CULPRIT.**
- Wants tonight (visible): the evening to run to time and take record money.
- Wants tonight (true): the pit empty by ten past ten.
- Secret: the skim. It is his motive, so for the "unrelated secret" slot: he hired a detective to catch a cash box pilferer who does not exist, to have a thief ready to blame.
- One lie: "I was at the cash table from Len's speech until I called the raffle." Refuted by any "grease on Dennis" fact, by Vic Lomas, and by slot B's own memory of minding the box.
- Discipline: everything else he says is true. "Bernard signed it off dry at six. I only announced what I was given." When caught he does not confess and does not tell a second lie: "I stepped away five minutes. A man can step away."

**Bernard Tolley, 58. Baths Superintendent since 1947, ex-Royal Navy stoker. Lives in the gate cottage.**
- Wants tonight: Len's signature on a letter keeping him in the cottage as caretaker.
- Unrelated secret: he has broken the hosepipe ban with most of a swimming pool, running it to the allotment society's tanks for marrows and a few pounds.
- One lie: "The pit was dry by six. I walked it with a broom." He lies because he signed the chit and because he believes his valve failed. Refuted by any T1 fact from someone else, or by the wide-open wheel.

**Miss Hilary Gaunt, 54. Vice-chairman. Games mistress at Tilverton High School for Girls; coach of Tilverton Otters.**
- Wants tonight: Len not to sign on Monday.
- Unrelated secret: she is "Naiad", who has been sending the District's committee papers to the Tilverton Advertiser. Carbons are in her kit bag.
- One lie: "I was at the committee table from eight until the last waltz. I have not been in that water since Thursday." Refuted by the damp costume and cap rolled in her towel. (Her damp hair is in the opening.)

**Clive Rudge, 38. Deputy Director of Leisure and Amenities, Harding Vale District Council.**
- Wants tonight: Len's signature on Monday, and tonight to pass without an incident on a District asset.
- Unrelated secret: he countersigned "tank empty" without looking, then went looking for breaches so the District could take the lido back without paying the Association a penny.
- One lie: "I remained on the terrace throughout." Refuted by his own clipboard, or by slot D's memory.

Witnesses, never suspects: Peggy Allsop, 63, tea urn. Vic Lomas, 59, bandleader.

## 9. Distribution at 2, 3 and 4 players

- Active slots: 2 players A and B; 3 players A, B, C; 4 players A, B, C, D.
- Every public opportunity is open to every active slot at every count. Interviews play on the TV (public claim). Searches and follow-ups return privately to the investigator's phone.
- Private starts give each slot one extra route: A to T2, B to half of T3, C and D to T1. No truth depends on a private route.
- Minimum new investigations to hold a full proof set: 4 for anyone using only public routes; 3 for each slot using its private start. Budget is 6.
- At 2 players the two private starts both lean toward Dennis (motive for A, a print for B), so neither rival begins ahead of the other.

## 10. Competitive window: "The Superintendent's Torch"

- Trigger (story): the first time any detective attempts a dark location (pit floor, board steps, sluice chamber, plant room). The festoons are off and Bernard owns the only good torch.
- Contest: one minigame, same challenge for everyone.
- Reward: the winner carries the torch and takes one extra, free investigation at any dark location, usable this round or next only. Everyone else still investigates those places at normal cost, by match and gate lamp, and finds the same facts.
- Access and priority only. No fact is exclusive to the winner.

## 13. Accepted reconstruction

Culprit plus a short arranged chain, as the GDD page requires. Not per-truth slots.

- Culprit: Dennis Wragg.
- Chain, in this order: "The pit was let out during the dance" then "Len made his closing-night dive off the top board".
- True optional pieces may be added without penalty: "The turnstile count and the banked takings disagree", "The books go to audit when the lease is surrendered".
- Any false piece in the chain fails it: "The pit was dry from six", "He walked off the edge in the dark", "Somebody turned a hose on him", "The old valve failed by itself", "He was drowned elsewhere and carried in".
- Proof: the player must own a complete route for T1, T2 and T3. Hearing a rival say it does not count.

## 15. Discarded tricks

1. **Drowned in the paddling pool and carried to the pit.** The first idea. It is the answer any player gives in ten seconds, it could happen at any pool in any year, and nothing seen earlier changes meaning.
2. **Len's speech was a recording over the Tannoy, shifting the time of death.** A manipulated-voice device already carries the v3.1 research case; repeating it would fail the freshness test, and tape tricks are stock.
3. **The body was kept in the ice cream freezer and thawed wet.** Time-of-death by cold is a stock move, leans on forensic claims I could not verify for 1976, and brushes against the tone rules.
4. **Pit truly dry; the killer hosed the body to wash off evidence.** Becomes a puzzle about who had a tap. Points at access, recasts nothing.
5. **The pit simply had not finished draining and he drowned.** Too close to "it was an accident all along", a banned move.

The kept trick came sixth, out of asking what "wet" could mean other than "water was added".

## 16. Checks against generic writing (run and recorded)

- Portability: every named detail was asked "could this move to another story unchanged?". Replaced: a bitumen-painted lid (any wet-paint story) became graphite grease on one spindle; a newspaper photographer's prints (any party) became the superintendent's only torch after the lights went off at the meter; a generic "embezzling treasurer" became a turnstile that counted a record summer the bank never saw; a generic "saving the pool" became a lease stranded by the 1974 council reorganisation.
- Names: chosen by birth year, Midlands shoe town and class. Leonard (1915), Bernard (1918), Hilary (1922, professional class), Peggy (1913), Dennis (1932), Clive (1938). "Miss Gaunt" keeps her title; Rudge uses twenty-four hour time.
- Lines read for cliche: cut "a shot rang out" style openers, "little did they know", every "suddenly", and one line in which a suspect "smiled, but the smile did not reach his eyes".
- Banned stock moves: none used. No twins, passages or poisons; the culprit speaks in the first minute; no confession is needed or given; the one piece of luck (Bernard's torch at 22:17) hurts the killer and is Bernard's routine, not chance; the false story is the accident, the truth is murder; the client twist is earned (section 8).
- Not the first idea: see section 15.
- Ordinary line for the ending: "He never looked down. Somebody knew that."
